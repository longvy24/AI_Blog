9 Prompts for When
AI Gives You Garbage

Bad output is rarely a bad model — it's a fixable ask. Nine prompts that fix it faster than rewriting the whole thing.

R
Ryu Vy
AIToolsDaily24 · By a human, for humans

You ask for something specific. It hands you something generic, or wrong, or subtly off. The instinct is to rewrite the whole prompt and try again — and usually that doesn't help either.

Bad output is rarely a bad model. It's an ask the model misread, or one that was too big, or a thread that's gone stale. These nine prompts fix the actual cause instead of rerolling and hoping. Every one is copy-paste.

The Nine

Every Fix, Copy-Paste Ready

01 / 09Diagnose
Ask Why It Missed
"That wasn't what I wanted. Before redoing it, tell me what you think I meant."
Surfaces the misread first. Nine times out of ten the output was fine for the wrong question — fix the misunderstanding, not the words.
02 / 09Constraint
Say What You Don't Want
"Redo it. Don't add intros, don't restructure, don't 'improve' my wording."
Naming what to avoid is faster than describing perfection three more times. Most “garbage” is the model helpfully adding things you never asked for.
03 / 09Example
Show It Good
"Here's an example of what good looks like: [paste]. Match this, not your last try."
One concrete example beats a paragraph of adjectives. It copies the pattern — structure, length, tone — instead of guessing what “better” means.
04 / 09Scope
Halve the Ask
"Forget the rest. Just nail the first part perfectly: [single piece]"
Garbage usually means the ask was too big. Shrink it until it can't miss, get that right, then build outward from a piece that works.
05 / 09Restart
Wipe the Slate
"Ignore everything above. Fresh start, new attempt, these rules only: [rules]"
A polluted thread drags every answer down — it keeps “remembering” the bad version. A clean restart beats patching a run that's already off the rails.
06 / 09Format
Name the Shape
"Return it as [table / 3 bullets / JSON] — exactly that shape, nothing around it."
“Make it cleaner” is vague and gets you vague. Name the exact container and it stops rambling in the margins.
07 / 09Verify
Make It Check Itself
"Before you answer, list the requirements. Then confirm your draft meets each one."
A self-check catches the missed instruction before it reaches you. It's the model proofreading against your own brief.
08 / 09Specifics
Numbers, Not Vibes
"Not 'short.' Give me exactly 40 words. Not 'formal.' No contractions, no slang."
Vague words get vague output. Hard numbers and explicit rules leave no room to drift — the difference between “ish” and exact.
09 / 09Stop
Cut Your Losses
"Third miss on the same thing? Stop. Give me an outline and I'll finish it."
Some tasks it won't land, and the fourth try rarely helps. Knowing when to take the wheel back is the actual skill — the outline still saves you the setup.
