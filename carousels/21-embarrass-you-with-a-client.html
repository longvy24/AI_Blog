8 Prompts So AI Doesn’t
Embarrass You With a Client

I once sent a summary with a confidently invented statistic in the third line. Nobody caught it in the meeting. Someone caught it after. These run before anything leaves my drafts.

I once sent out a summary with a confidently invented statistic sitting in the third line. Nobody caught it in the meeting. Someone caught it afterwards — which is worse.

These eight prompts are what I run before anything client-facing leaves my drafts folder. They hunt for the confident-but-wrong before someone else does. Copy-paste.

The Eight

Every Check, Before You Send

01 / 08Audit
Find What's Wrong First
"Before I send this: what in here is factually wrong, unverifiable, or invented?"
Far cheaper to find it now than to be corrected in the meeting.
02 / 08Accuracy
Flag Every Number
"List every figure, date and name. Mark which ones you'd bet money on."
The number is what gets checked, and what gets remembered.
03 / 08Voice
Strip the Tells
"Remove anything that sounds like AI wrote it. No 'delve', no 'tapestry', no rule of three."
Your reader has seen a thousand of these this year. They can tell.
04 / 08Read
Read It Back As Them
"Read this as my client. Where do they push back, and where do they stop reading?"
You can't see your own draft cold. It can.
05 / 08Risk
Name the Worst Line
"What's the single sentence most likely to get me in trouble?"
There is always one, and you are far too close to spot it.
06 / 08Honesty
Say What You Don't Know
"Where did you fill a gap because I didn't give you the information?"
A confident guess inside a client document is the expensive kind of wrong.
07 / 08Tone
Match the Register
"Rewrite for someone who has paid me for six years and has no time."
Over-explaining reads as junior. Under-explaining reads as careless.
08 / 08Gate
Would You Send This?
"If your name was on this, would you send it? What would you fix first?"
The last question before you hit send shouldn't be your own.
