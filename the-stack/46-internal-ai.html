The 22-second version · Stage 2 of 5

Stage two. The company I work for had built its own AI tooling, and in the early days it was rough. The detail that turned it into a lesson instead of a complaint: I couldn't just switch to something better. This was the tool. So the only move left was to get good at getting work out of it.

What I found is that a weak tool doesn't need cleverer prompts. It needs smaller ones. Everything below is a way of shrinking the ask until the tool can't get it wrong.

The whole method
Halve the ask.

The Method

Shrink The Ask Until It Fits

A capable model can hold a multi-step request in its head and keep the thread. A weak one compounds a small slip at step one into a broken answer by step four. The fix isn't a smarter prompt — it's fewer steps per prompt, and a hard rule that nothing gets to make a claim without showing its receipt.

01 / 04Split
One Verb Per Prompt
"Do exactly one thing: [single action]. Nothing else. Stop when it's done."
A weak model loses the thread across a multi-part ask. One verb per prompt means one thing to get right and one thing to check. Chain the steps yourself — don't make the tool hold the chain.
02 / 04Bound
Cap The Output
"Answer in three bullets, twelve words each. No preamble, no summary."
Unbounded output is where a rough tool wanders. A hard cap forces it to pick the load-bearing point instead of padding around it. Tight limits are a feature, not a constraint — they do the editing the tool won't.
03 / 04Cite
A Quoted Source, Or It's Gone
"Every claim needs a quoted line from the source. No quote, cut the claim."
The single rule that saved me the most rework. On a tool you don't fully trust, an unsourced claim is a guess wearing a fact's clothes. Make it show the quote or drop the line — anything that couldn't cite, I deleted.
04 / 04Strip
Kill The Preamble
"Skip the intro. Your first line is the answer itself."
Preamble is where a weak model warms up and drifts. Cutting it isn't just tidier — it forces the tool to commit to a real answer on line one, which is exactly where you can judge it fastest.
// What the rough tool taught me

None of this was the tool I'd have chosen, and I won't pretend the early version was good. But being stuck with it taught me discipline a capable tool lets you skip. Halving the ask, bounding the output, demanding a source — these still work on the best model I've used since. They're just optional there, and mandatory on a tool you didn't pick and can't leave.
